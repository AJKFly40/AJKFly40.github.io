Reference

Glossary

Every column on a season page, what measured it, and — the field that matters — what it cannot be used to claim.

Record

Rk rank

Measures
Final or current position in the regular-season table, after the league's own tiebreakers.
Who measured it
Sleeper, via wkp.stats.standings.regular_season_standings.
Population and era
Regular-season weeks only. Eight of twelve reach the playoffs; the bottom four enter the draft lottery.
What it cannot say
Rank is not a measure of how well a roster played. That is what the all-play record and expected wins are for: checked against 2025, Dana Kirschner finished 8th by rank but 4th by all-play rate, a four-place gap and the largest in the league that season.

Manager manager_name

Measures
The manager credited with this roster this season, by their canonical name.
Who measured it
managers.canonical_name, joined through teams by wkp.stats.standings.regular_season_standings.
Population and era
Every roster in a full-fidelity season (2018-2025). A curated season's stated table carries the same name from a different source, archive_season_records.
What it cannot say
It is not necessarily today's owner of this history -- the manager on record for a season stays attached to that season's row even after they have left the league or sat one out, the same real gaps managers.json's own seasons list carries (Josh Mleczko played 2014, sat out 2015-2016, and returned in 2017 as three separate facts, not one span).

W wins

Measures
Regular-season wins, one decision a week against the roster's actual scheduled opponent.
Who measured it
wkp.stats.standings.regular_season_standings, tallied from matchups.
Population and era
Regular season, full-fidelity era (2018-2025) only. A curated 2014-2017 season shows the commissioner's own stated win total instead of a sum over an incomplete game log.
What it cannot say
It says nothing about the schedule that produced it -- a roster can lead the league here and still trail on All-play, which is the same roster measured against every opponent instead of the one they actually drew. See Luck for the gap between the two.

L losses

Measures
Regular-season losses, one decision a week against the roster's actual scheduled opponent.
Who measured it
wkp.stats.standings.regular_season_standings, tallied from matchups.
Population and era
Regular season, full-fidelity era (2018-2025) only. Checked against the vault: zero ties across the era's 654 paired regular-season games, so W and L always account for every decision; a tie, were one ever to occur, would count as half a win in Pct without moving either column.
What it cannot say
It is not a verdict on the lineup they set in those weeks -- see Shd-W for how many of these losses their own best available lineup would have overturned.

Pct pct

Measures
Win percentage over the regular season, with a tie counted as half a win.
Who measured it
SeasonRow.pct, a property computed from wins, losses and ties in wkp.stats.standings_columns.
Population and era
Regular season, full-fidelity era (2018-2025) only.
What it cannot say
It is the record the actual schedule produced, not a rate of skill -- AP% is the identical statistic computed against the whole league instead of the one opponent drawn each week, and is what this column should be checked against before reading anything into it.

GB games_back

Measures
Games behind the standings leader, by the standard formula: ((leader's wins minus this roster's wins) plus (this roster's losses minus the leader's losses)) divided by two.
Who measured it
wkp.stats.standings_columns._games_back.
Population and era
Regular season, full-fidelity era (2018-2025) only.
What it cannot say
It answers 'how far back', never 'how deserving' -- a roster can trail here while leading All-play, because this is counted off the actual record the schedule produced, the exact thing All-play and Luck exist to check.

Strk streak

Measures
This roster's current or final run of consecutive wins or losses, e.g. '3W' or '2L'.
Who measured it
teams.current_streak, read straight through by wkp.stats.standings_columns._activity.
Population and era
Null for every roster in 2014-2020 and for the not-yet-played 2026 season -- Sleeper started surfacing this field to the league later than it started surfacing Waiver Position, measured against the live vault. A real value for every roster 2021-2025.
What it cannot say
It describes a run of results, not a level of play -- a long streak can end the very next week, and it carries no information about the schedule or the lineup behind it.

Scoring

PF points_for

Measures
Total points this roster scored across the regular season.
Who measured it
wkp.stats.standings.regular_season_standings, summed from matchups.
Population and era
Regular season, full-fidelity era (2018-2025) only. A curated season shows the commissioner's own stated total, never a sum over its incomplete log.
What it cannot say
On its own it says nothing about whether those points were deployed well -- a high total paired with a low Eff means the roster scored a lot and still left more on the bench than most. See PP and Eff for that half of the story.

PA points_against

Measures
Total points this roster's scheduled opponents scored across the regular season, from the same games.
Who measured it
wkp.stats.standings_columns._points_against, off the opponent side of the same game log PF reads.
Population and era
Regular season, full-fidelity era (2018-2025) only.
What it cannot say
It is a fact about the schedule this roster was handed, not about the roster itself -- pairing it with All-play (the record against every roster, not just the one drawn) is what separates the two.

Diff point_differential

Measures
Points for minus points against, across the regular season.
Who measured it
SeasonRow.point_differential, a property computed from PF and PA.
Population and era
Regular season, full-fidelity era (2018-2025) only.
What it cannot say
It is not the same ordering as the standings, which are sorted on wins first -- a roster can lead here while trailing in the table, because one blowout win and a string of narrow ones bank identical win totals off very different differentials.

PPG points_per_game

Measures
Points for divided by the number of regular-season weeks this roster actually played.
Who measured it
wkp.stats.standings_columns.season_rows.
Population and era
Regular season, full-fidelity era (2018-2025) only. Null for a roster with zero weeks played -- an average of nothing is not a zero.
What it cannot say
It smooths over the season's shape -- a roster that started slow and finished strong reads identically here to one that did the reverse. See Hi, Lo and SD for the shape a single average erases.

Hi high

Measures
This roster's own highest single-week score in the regular season.
Who measured it
wkp.stats.standings_columns.season_rows, off that roster's own weekly scores.
Population and era
Regular season, full-fidelity era (2018-2025) only. Null for a roster with zero weeks played.
What it cannot say
It is the best week this roster actually posted, not the best it could have -- that ceiling is PP, built from the optimal lineup rather than the one that was actually started. A high Hi does not mean a lineup was well managed; it may just mean the starters got hot on their own.

Lo low

Measures
This roster's own lowest single-week score in the regular season.
Who measured it
wkp.stats.standings_columns.season_rows, off that roster's own weekly scores.
Population and era
Regular season, full-fidelity era (2018-2025) only. Null for a roster with zero weeks played.
What it cannot say
It is not evidence of a lineup mistake -- a low week can come from byes and injuries thinning the roster just as easily as from a bad decision at kickoff. See PP and Bench for whether more points were actually available that week.

SD stdev

Measures
Population standard deviation of this roster's own regular-season weekly scores -- how much a single week typically swings from its own average.
Who measured it
statistics.pstdev over the roster's own weekly scores, in wkp.stats.standings_columns.season_rows.
Population and era
Regular season, full-fidelity era (2018-2025) only. Null for a roster with one or fewer weeks played -- a spread needs at least two points.
What it cannot say
It says nothing about whether the swings were good or bad luck -- a high spread here describes the shape of the scores, not a claim that the roster over- or under-performed. For that, read Luck or Pyth.

Was it deserved?

All-play all_play_wins

Measures
This roster's record if it had played every other roster, every week of the regular season, instead of just the one it was actually scheduled against.
Who measured it
wkp.stats.luck.all_play.
Population and era
Regular season, full-fidelity era (2018-2025) only, floored to complete-field weeks. 143 comparisons a roster-season in 2018-2020 (13 weeks x 11 opponents), 154 from 2021 (14 x 11) -- measured against the live vault.
What it cannot say
It is not a claim about how well a manager played -- a roster can be unlucky and genuinely bad at once, and this column says nothing about the lineup they set. See Eff and Shd-W for that.

AP% all_play_pct

Measures
All-play wins, with a tie worth half, as a share of all-play comparisons -- the same rate as the All-play column, expressed so it can be ranked without carrying its own denominator.
Who measured it
wkp.stats.luck.AllPlaySeason.all_play_pct.
Population and era
Regular season, full-fidelity era (2018-2025) only, floored to complete-field weeks, same population as All-play.
What it cannot say
It is the rate, not the record -- pair it with the All-play column to see how many games it is built from, and see Luck for what the gap to the real record says about the schedule rather than the roster.

xW expected_wins

Measures
The wins this roster's all-play rate was worth, over a season of this many games -- all-play percentage times games played.
Who measured it
wkp.stats.luck.AllPlaySeason.expected_wins.
Population and era
Regular season, full-fidelity era (2018-2025) only.
What it cannot say
It is not a prediction and nothing was ever wagered on it -- it exists to be compared against the real win total (see W) to produce Luck, and on its own it says nothing about the actual schedule that was drawn.

Luck luck

Measures
Actual wins minus expected wins. Negative means the schedule cost them games they would have won against an average opponent.
Who measured it
Derived here from wkp.stats.luck.all_play, which is computed from every roster's weekly score.
Population and era
Regular-season weeks only, every roster against every other, so a fourteen-week season is 154 comparisons per roster.
What it cannot say
It is not a claim about how a manager played. A roster can be unlucky and bad at once, and luck says nothing about the lineup they set -- for that, read Efficiency and Shd-W.

Median median_wins

Measures
This roster's record for the season against the league's own weekly median score, one decision a week regardless of how many rosters reported. A score landing exactly on that median counts as a loss, not a win -- at this league's twelve-team field the median is the mean of the 6th and 7th scores, which only ever equals a real score on a tie for the middle of the field, and that has not happened in any of the 109 full-fidelity regular-season weeks 2018-2025 (wkp.stats.standings_columns._median_and_top has the count and the convention's full reasoning).
Who measured it
wkp.stats.standings_columns._median_and_top.
Population and era
Regular season, full-fidelity era (2018-2025) only. This league does not use median scoring -- the figure is hypothetical, what the standings would show under a format this league has never played.
What it cannot say
It cannot be added to W and L to reconcile the real standings -- it is a separate, parallel record under different rules, not a correction to the actual one.

Pyth pythagorean_wins

Measures
The wins this roster's points for and against were worth, under an exponent (5.478) fitted to this league's own scoring distribution rather than borrowed from baseball's 1.83 or football's 2.37.
Who measured it
wkp.stats.luck.pythagorean, at PYTHAGOREAN_EXPONENT.
Population and era
Regular season, full-fidelity era (2018-2025) only.
What it cannot say
A gap between this and W describes one season, never a property of the person: the year-to-year correlation of a manager's residual is r = +0.095 (p = 0.39, 82 consecutive pairs), so no single season's gap here should be read as a repeatable skill. See wkp.stats.luck.persistence_p for the one figure that survives a test, and by how little.

Top top_weeks

Measures
Weeks this roster held the league's single highest score that week, ties for the top sharing credit.
Who measured it
wkp.stats.standings_columns._median_and_top.
Population and era
Regular season, full-fidelity era (2018-2025) only.
What it cannot say
It is not a general measure of scoring well -- a roster that finishes a close second every single week can outscore the league across the season while leading Top zero times, because this counts weeks WON, not points scored relative to the field. See PF and PPG for the totals it cannot see.

The lineup they set

PP potential_points

Measures
The most this roster's own players could have scored, across the regular season, playing the best legal lineup available every week.
Who measured it
wkp.stats.potential.week_potentials, an exact lineup optimiser validated against Sleeper's own potential_points, summed by wkp.stats.standings_columns.season_rows.
Population and era
Regular season, full-fidelity era (2018-2025) only.
What it cannot say
It is a ceiling built from a lineup nobody actually set, and in one roster-week the vault holds (2021 week 14, a position eligibility Sleeper has since overwritten) the optimiser could not reproduce the real lineup, so the figure was a floor rather than a true ceiling that week. It is not an accusation: the optimal lineup is knowable only after the week is over.

Eff efficiency

Measures
Points actually scored divided by the points the best available lineup would have scored, across the regular season -- points for over potential points on the SEASON totals.
Who measured it
wkp.stats.standings_columns.season_rows: this season's PF divided by PP, as a percentage. This is MyFantasyLeague's own 'Eff' definition -- its help text reads 'Eff (Efficiency) — Points For divided by Potential Points, expressed as percentage.'
Population and era
Regular season, full-fidelity era (2018-2025) only. Null when PP itself is null or zero (no weeks played).
What it cannot say
It cannot say they set a good lineup in a typical week, only that they captured their points overall -- a single disastrous week that also happened to be their biggest costs them more here than it would in Eff/wk. wkp.newsroom.potential_facts makes the identical choice at the career level (its luck.efficiency fact): checked against 2025, this figure and Eff/wk differ by up to 0.76 points and swap the column's order -- Matt Thoman and Mark Kirschner cross around Todd Lambert, Josh Mleczko and Tom Gleason cross around Ian Hendry. See Eff/wk for how evenly the points were captured week to week.

Eff/wk weekly_efficiency

Measures
The mean of this roster's own weekly (actual over potential) ratios -- every week counts once, regardless of how many points it was worth.
Who measured it
wkp.stats.potential.WeekPotential.efficiency, averaged across the regular season by wkp.stats.standings_columns._lineup_totals.
Population and era
Regular season, full-fidelity era (2018-2025) only. A week whose potential is zero is excluded from the average rather than counted as a 0% week; null if every week was.
What it cannot say
It cannot say they captured a lot of points, only that they are consistent about trying to -- a manager can lead this column and still have left the most points on the bench in the league, because a 60-point week and a 160-point week count exactly the same here. Eff instead weights each week by its size. Checked against 2025, Justin Poe is the widest gap in the league: 85.0% on Eff against 85.7% here, meaning his biggest misses landed on his biggest weeks.

Bench bench_points

Measures
Points scored by players who were on the roster but not started, summed across the regular season.
Who measured it
wkp.stats.potential.WeekPotential.left_on_bench, summed by wkp.stats.standings_columns._lineup_totals.
Population and era
Regular season, full-fidelity era (2018-2025) only.
What it cannot say
It does not say a manager made a mistake -- the best lineup is knowable only after the week, and a big bench total can just as easily mean a deep, well-drafted roster as a bad decision at kickoff.

Shd-W should_have_won

Measures
Weeks they lost in which the best lineup available to them that week would have outscored what their opponent actually scored.
Who measured it
wkp.stats.potential -- the same optimiser Sleeper's own potential_points validated, which caught two wrong implementations before this one.
Population and era
Regular-season weeks only (this column never looks at the playoffs) where the lineup reproduced exactly. All 168 of 2025's regular-season roster-weeks (12 rosters x 14 weeks) did.
What it cannot say
It is not a count of mistakes anybody could have avoided. The optimal lineup is known only afterwards; this measures what was on the roster, not what was knowable on the Sunday.

Cld-L could_have_lost

Measures
Weeks they won that the opponent's own best available lineup would have beaten what they actually scored -- they got away with one.
Who measured it
wkp.stats.potential.WeekPotential.could_have_lost, counted by wkp.stats.standings_columns._lineup_totals.
Population and era
Regular-season weeks only (this column never looks at the playoffs) where the lineup reproduced exactly. All 168 of 2025's regular-season roster-weeks (12 rosters x 14 weeks) did.
What it cannot say
It is not a claim that the win was undeserved -- the opponent never actually set that lineup either. This compares one side's potential against the other's actual, never potential against potential, because that fixture never existed.

Activity

Moves moves

Measures
Completed transactions naming this roster this season -- adds, drops and trades. A trade counts once for each roster it names.
Who measured it
wkp.stats.standings_columns._moves, filtered to status = 'complete'. A failed waiver claim moved nothing and does not count, by the commissioner's own ruling.
Population and era
Null before 2018 -- transactions holds zero rows for the whole archive era, a gap in what was recorded rather than a season nobody transacted in.
What it cannot say
teams.total_moves must never be read in its place -- it is a stale Sleeper counter that reads 0 for every 2025 roster while this column's own twelve numbers sum to 255 completed moves that season. (2025's raw transactions table holds 408 rows; 248 completed and 160 failed waiver claims this column correctly excludes -- 408 is a count of attempts, never what this column counts.) A confident zero here would be exactly the failure this project treats as worse than a missing number.

FAAB faab_used

Measures
Dollars of this roster's waiver budget spent on winning claims this season.
Who measured it
teams.waiver_budget_used, read straight through by wkp.stats.standings_columns._activity.
Population and era
Null for every 2014-2017 roster -- Sleeper did not yet track a waiver budget for those seasons. A real integer for every 2018-2025 roster, measured against the live vault.
What it cannot say
It says nothing about whether the spending worked -- a big FAAB total is activity, not a verdict on the players it bought.

WPos waiver_position

Measures
This roster's position in the current waiver order, 1 being first priority.
Who measured it
teams.waiver_position, read straight through by wkp.stats.standings_columns._activity.
Population and era
Null for every 2014-2017 roster, a real 1-12 for every 2018-2025 roster, measured against the live vault.
What it cannot say
It is a queue position on a given day, not a ranking of the manager -- in a rolling-priority league it moves every time a claim is won, so it says nothing about the season as a whole.
